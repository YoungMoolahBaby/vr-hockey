Scope note: research done 2026-11-07. Several Sense Arena official pages carry no publication date, only a "(c) 2026" footer; their dates are given as "page live 2026". Vendor claims are marked **[VENDOR]**. Independent press, users, coaches and researchers are marked **[INDEPENDENT]**. Partner or sponsored voices, such as team executives or players named on vendor pages, are marked **[PARTNER]**. Items seen only in a search-result snippet (the full page could not be fetched) are marked **[SNIPPET]**.

## 1. Sense Arena / NHL Sense Arena: modules, drills, session structure, progression, metrics, pricing, hardware, adoption

### Takeaway
NHL Sense Arena (Sense Arena LLC, launched in 2018 as a goalie trainer) is the clear market leader and the only VR hockey trainer licensed by the NHL. As of 2026 ("NHL Sense Arena 27" on the Quest store) it is a freemium Quest app with:
- a subscription of about $29/mo billed annually ($348/yr) or $59/mo month to month;
- a leveled goalie program (GAP), a player course series (PAS), and a "Playmaker Mode" with a 4-part Hockey IQ Score;
- DanglePro, a mixed-reality real-stick, real-puck stickhandling module for Quest 3/3S;
- a full-ice 3v3 NHL season game.

Its outcome numbers are vendor averages with no method published. Its adoption claims (USA Hockey official tool, 17 NCAA D1 programs, 5 NHL team contracts, 450-500k users) come from press releases.

**Session structure and progression** [VENDOR] — [How progressive training works](https://hockey.sensearena.com/resources/off-ice-drills/cognitive-drills/how-progressive-training-works-article)
- **Goalie Advancement Program (GAP)** structure:
  - 3 focus areas: Angles, Managing Traffic, Reading a Release
  - 8 levels per focus area, with 3 training days per level
  - 6 to 8 drills per day, about 8 to 10 minutes each
  - selectable easy, medium and hard difficulty
  - each level builds on the one before
- **Player Advancement Series (PAS)**: guided courses of drill sequences of rising difficulty. Areas: scanning, anticipation, puck retrievals, reading opponents, and decisions with limited time and space. The page calls it both "Player Advanced Skills" and "Player Advancement Series".
- **Drill library counts by category**: **Forwards 45, Defense 14, Goalies 54, Cognitive 18**, plus a full-ice 3-on-3 mode for scanning, passing and off-puck movement.
- Engagement layer: training objectives, competitions and challenges that earn **XP**; curated training plans "from experts and NHL pros"; "USA Hockey-approved" drills and plans — [players page](https://hockey.sensearena.com/players)
- **Leaderboards** rank drill results against other users. In Aug 2024, multiplayer let friends compete on separate headsets but could not show each other's progress; a "north-south" mode was "in development" — [NHL.com 2024](https://nhl.com/news/nhl-sense-arena-virtual-reality-tool-on-display-at-fanatics-fest-nyc)

### Inferences
- Sense Arena's design follows a clear template:
  - short drills, roughly 8-10 min each
  - leveled multi-day programs (GAP: 3 areas x 8 levels x 3 days)
  - game-like wrappers (NHL licence, season mode, 3v3) to drive retention
  - a single composite "Hockey IQ Score" (decisions, scanning, reading, reaction) as the headline metric

  A new entrant will be compared against this template.
- Content weighting favours goalies (54 drills) and forwards (45) over **defence (14)**, which leaves defencemen underserved. Goalies are the historical core: the product began as a goalie trainer, and independent voices most often call it valuable for goalies.
- DanglePro and "full-ice scanning" show Sense Arena already moving into "real puck plus awareness". The mixed-reality route uses a real puck because controller-simulated puck physics drew complaints (see section 3). A new entrant claiming "physics-real puck plus decisions" would have to beat DanglePro specifically.
- The outcome claims (+10.6% save %, +9% decision speed) are the main marketing lever. They have no published method, so an entrant with transparent, validated metrics could differentiate.

### Gaps
- The full list of the 100+ drills and the per-drill scoring or metrics could not be retrieved. Only five skater drills are named publicly.
- How the Hockey IQ Score is computed (scale, weighting, norms by age) is not published.
- Whether difficulty adapts per user automatically (beyond the easy/medium/hard choice and leveled courses) is not documented. The store copy mentions "adaptive training" for DanglePro without details.
- I found no description of a coach or team dashboard in 2026. The 2021 cloud platform "for players and coaches" was mentioned without details.
- Prices of the stick mount and goalie sleeves were not found.
- The vendor's ".cloudfront.net" mirror and some sensearena.com resource pages could not be fetched (DNS errors or 404s), so some vendor details come from search snippets only.

## 2. Other products: what they train and measure

### Takeaway
No other dedicated VR hockey-sense trainer of comparable depth was found. The rest of the market splits into five groups:
- (a) hobbyist VR hockey games focused on stick and puck physics or shooting (Hockey VR, VR Hockey Reborn, several Steam early-access titles), with no cognitive curriculum
- (b) screen-based cognitive trainers: Hockey IntelliGym (USA Hockey), NeuroTracker (generic 3D multiple-object tracking), and Reflexion light boards
- (c) video "pause and decide" hockey-IQ apps and courses
- (d) wearables that measure skating, not sense (Helios)
- (e) analogues in other sports: WIN Reality in baseball, which trains pitch recognition and swing decisions with explicit benchmarks; Rezzil in soccer and other sports

### Cited Findings

**VR hockey games (consumer, physics-focused)**
- **Hockey VR** (Melcher Studios, Steam, $4.99, released 14 Oct 2021):
  - Unreal-based puck and stick physics with two-controller puck handling
  - modes: Timed Shot, Shot Count and One-Timer from 4-5 ice spots; target shooting or play against an AI goalie
  - Shot Assist, left/right sticks, a local leaderboard, and an optional "Realstick" mount for a real stick
  - PC VR only (Quest via Link); no multiplayer; only 7 user reviews, too few for a score

  — [Steam: Hockey VR](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1769010/Hockey_VR)
- **VR Hockey Reborn** (solo developer Vladislav Prygin, Quest, free, released 6 Jun 2022, v0.0.42):
  - "realistic puck possession physics", built by a former player, "tested by real hockey players"
  - multiplayer lobbies, playable goalie with animated butterfly pads, one-handed play
  - rating **4.0 from 434 ratings / 299 reviews**

  — [QuestStoreDB: VR Hockey Reborn](https://queststoredb.com/game/vr-hockey-reborn/5307901462582204)
- No VR hockey game found here offers decision-making curricula, scanning metrics or progression plans. They focus on shooting, possession physics and multiplayer — [Steam: Hockey VR](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1769010/Hockey_VR); [QuestStoreDB: VR Hockey Reborn](https://queststoredb.com/game/vr-hockey-reborn/5307901462582204)

**Screen-based cognitive trainers**
- **Hockey IntelliGym** (developed with USA Hockey):
  - a video-game-like program presenting cognitive scenarios similar to on-ice play
  - claimed targets: perception, anticipation, decisions under pressure, spatial awareness, pattern recognition

  — [Wikipedia: IntelliGym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IntelliGym)
- IntelliGym levels grow harder by adding constraints, such as a minimum number of passes before shooting or partially hidden screen elements [SNIPPET] — [The Hockey Writers](https://thehockeywriters.com/intelligym-helping-develop-hockey-sense/)
- **University of Delaware controlled study (2021)**: 22 U16/U18 male players; 2x30-min IntelliGym sessions per week for 20 weeks versus a control group watching hockey video. Outcome measure: head-impact kinematics. Reporting describes a significant drop in the number and magnitude of head impacts in the trained group [INDEPENDENT academic, small n] — [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/372985826.pdf)
- USA Hockey NTDP injury analysis (2017-19 versus 2019-20) reported fewer injury-related doctor visits and head injuries after adopting IntelliGym. It was authored by IntelliGym's research director (company-linked, needs replication) — [IntelliGym: How Hockey Sense Training Changed the Game](https://usahockeyintelligym.com/?p=747)
- **NeuroTracker** (3D multiple-object tracking; not hockey-specific):
  - The vendor claims research with two NHL teams showed "dramatic learning responses" [VENDOR, no full citation] — [NeuroTracker hockey use case](https://www.neurotrackerx.com/use-cases/hockey)
  - **Independent, negative for talent ID**: a study of 35 Canadian players aged 16-20 found baseline NeuroTracker scores did not predict draft ranking or game statistics, though they correlated with working-memory and attention tests — [Research Quarterly for Exercise and Sport, 2023](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02701367.2023.2216266)
  - Among 136 varsity athletes, hockey players scored higher than athletes from other sports — [Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1372350/pdf)
  - A Mitacs project is testing 18 weeks of NeuroTracker for hockey performance; I found no published results — [Mitacs](https://www.mitacs.ca/our-projects/efficacy-of-a-perceptive-cognitive-training-to-improve-ice-hockey-performance/)
- **Reflexion Edge**: a portable LED touchscreen light board for peripheral vision and reaction time. Its named pro work is with **MLB** (eight teams); I found no evidence of NHL use — [Reflexion for coaches](https://edge.reflexion.co/); [Reflexion Edge product](https://shop.reflexion.co/products/edge)

The library is updated with shift breakdowns and lessons [SNIPPET, vendor] — [The Coaches Site: Project Hockey IQ](https://members.thecoachessite.com/article/cale-makar-hockey-iq-game-2.0-%7C-project-hockey-iq)

**Analogues in other sports (structure templates)**
- **WIN Reality** (baseball/softball VR) [VENDOR]:
  - trains **pitch recognition, timing and swing-decision accuracy**, explicitly not swing mechanics, and says it does not replace live at-bats or cage work
  - publishes a concrete benchmark: calling pitch type correctly **70%+ of the time before the pitch's halfway point** means recognition is improving
  - tracks bat speed, pitch location and path, 3D strike zone, contact point, swing path and hit path
  - warns that virtual exit velocity is "directional" only

  — [WIN Reality: Does VR baseball training work?](https://winreality.com/?p=185404); [WIN Reality: What is pitch recognition](https://winreality.com/blog/what-is-pitch-recognition-a-hitters-guide/)
- WIN's own research summary admits that both the VR group and a regular-practice group improved on a video pitch-recognition test, so VR was not clearly superior on that test — [WIN Reality](https://winreality.com/?p=185404)
- In Dec 2025 WIN acquired Yakkertech and launched "SmartPark", ballpark tracking tech, extending VR into on-field data — [BusinessWire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20251216500962/en/WIN-Acquires-Yakkertech-and-Launches-SmartPark-Unlocking-Pro-Caliber-Tech-for-Every-Diamond)
- **Rezzil**: VR cognitive and technical training mainly for soccer and basketball. "Rezzil Player" has 160+ repeatable drills for reaction speed and skills. I found no hockey product — [Soccerscene](https://soccerscene.com.au/rezzil-vr-integrating-virtual-reality-in-sports/)

### Inferences
- Sense Arena's only VR competition in hockey is hobbyist physics games. Its real competitors for "hockey sense" spend are cheaper screen and video tools (IntelliGym, Project Hockey IQ) and generic cognitive trainers (NeuroTracker).
- WIN Reality is the best structural template from another sport:
  - narrow, named cognitive targets (recognize, then decide)
  - transparent benchmarks (70% before the halfway point)
  - honest framing of what VR doesn't train
  - a move toward linking with real-game data

  A hockey analogue could be "read pressure / identify the open option within X ms of reception" benchmarks, linked to game video.
- Independent evidence for generic cognitive trainers in hockey is weak. NeuroTracker failed to predict hockey performance in the 2023 study, so sport-specific, representative-task VR has a credible scientific angle over generic MOT.

### Gaps
- Several Steam VR hockey titles surfaced in searches without verifiable store-page details: "Hockey Player VR" (reportedly "Mixed", about 42 reviews, early-access alpha), "VR Skills Hockey" (reportedly "Mostly Negative", 10 reviews, with a user saying stick/puck physics need improvement) and "VR Hockey League" ("realistic physics of possession"). Their app IDs and review text were not confirmed.
- I found no dedicated VR goalie trainer from a vendor other than Sense Arena.
- IntelliGym's current (2026) pricing, platform and metric outputs could not be fetched (the vendor site's SSL failed).
- No data was found on the "VGK VR" initiative (Vegas Golden Knights) or on the University of Wisconsin-Stevens Point VR injury project's results.
- I found no reliable evidence that Rezzil or Beyond Sports offer hockey-specific products.

## 3. What players, parents and coaches say (praise and complaints)

### Takeaway
Independent feedback is thin but consistent. Praise centres on:
- realism of video shooters for goalies
- safe repetition of decisions
- convenience on days without ice

Complaints centre on:
- **cost** (subscription plus headset plus mounts)
- **controller-based puck control** (the puck "skittles under my stick")
- no skating or physical contact feedback
- low team-level adoption
- billing and cancellation friction

### Inferences
- The strongest product-market fit is for **goalies** (video shooters and release reading) and for **cognitive and decision reps**. The weakest fit is **skater hands with controllers**, which is why Sense Arena added the real-puck DanglePro.
- Cost sensitivity is the dominant barrier for families. Most users are U16/U18 per the vendor, so parents are the payers, and a lower-cost or clearly evidence-backed offer would address the main objection.
- Trust gaps include unverifiable % improvement claims and cancellation friction. A competitor with transparent metrics and easy billing could exploit them.

## 4. Missing features across the market that a new trainer could own

### Takeaway
Based on the evidence above, the clearest openings are:
- **defenceman-specific** decision content (only 14 Sense Arena defence drills)
- **physics-real puck handling fused with game decisions** in full VR (controller puck control is criticized; DanglePro is mixed-reality, Quest 3/3S-only and stickhandling-centred)
- **transparent, validated metrics** with true adaptive difficulty, versus vendor % claims, an undisclosed IQ-score formula and manual easy/medium/hard
- **team systems and coach tooling**: no team-systems curriculum (breakouts, forecheck structures) or coach dashboard is evident in any product
- **lower total cost** and friction-free billing

Skating and locomotion realism remains unsolved by everyone.

### Inferences
- **Defenceman-first trainer.** No product centres the D-man reads. These include gap control, retrievals under forecheck pressure, D-to-D versus rim versus stretch decisions, shot-through-traffic lanes and pinch decisions. Sense Arena's 14 defence drills against 45 forward drills suggests this is a real hole.
- **Physics-real puck plus decisions in one rep, in full VR.** The opportunity is to make reception, handling, a scan and a pass happen in one continuous rep, with credible puck and stick physics. This avoids the separate DanglePro-style mixed-reality module and the dependence on Quest 3/3S passthrough. The rep would be measured as one chain (scan before reception, then decision latency, then execution quality).
- **Evidence-grade, transparent metrics.** Examples:
  - pre-reception scan count and timing (shoulder checks, from head yaw)
  - decision latency from puck arrival
  - option quality scored against an expert or model "best option"
  - published, explainable scoring with age norms

  Pair this with true per-user adaptive difficulty (staircase on time pressure, defender pressure and occlusion) rather than manual tiers. This would directly counter the main trust gap.
- **Team systems and coach layer.** The opening is a coach-authored systems mode: load a team's breakout or forecheck structures and have players rehearse their roles and reads. Add a coach dashboard across a roster. This would target team purchases, where adoption is currently "individual".
- **Price and positioning.** Lower cost, or a headset-optional companion (video or 2D mode), plus honest "what this trains / doesn't train" framing in WIN Reality's style. This addresses the parent-payer objection and builds credibility.
- **Skating realism** is a market-wide gap, but likely hardware-limited. An entrant can stay away from it and state the limitation clearly.

### Gaps
- No market sizing (number of paying subscribers, revenue) for Sense Arena or competitors was found.
- I couldn't confirm whether Sense Arena has a hidden team or club license with a coach dashboard; the public pages do not describe one.
- No independent controlled trial of any VR hockey trainer (Sense Arena included) showing on-ice performance transfer was found. The Fribourg study is lab perception with a single session.
- I didn't check whether any product does defenceman-specific skating-path or gap-control training in VR.
